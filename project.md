## What to do

Your project can be any of these:

- Apply an existing AI/ML method to a scientific problem, especially one from your own dissertation. 
- Develop a new AI/ML method for an open scientific challenge. 
- Reproduce a recent AI-in-science paper with your own implementation, then go beyond it by adding something (e.g., a new dataset, a failure case, an ablation, a stronger baseline). 
- Write a literature review of an emerging area at the AI/ML--science intersection. LLMs can write literature reviews and a project that goes in this direction should demonstrate something that goes well beyond what an LLM can do on its own. (Caution: this topic is easy to do poorly but hard to do well because LLMs are so competitive.)

I especially like projects that could turn into a research paper. If I waived your homework (see the [syllabus](syllabus.html#homework)), the project replaces it and I'll expect more.

Work alone or in a team of two. A team should do more. If you work in a team, add a section to the project report explaining who did what. Each team member is required to give a substantial part of the presentation. 

## <a name="proposal"></a>Proposal --- Fri, Nov 6

Half a page to one page, as a PDF on Gradescope. One per team, with both names. Include:

1. The problem, and why it matters.
2. The data or simulator, and whether you already have access to it. If you don't, say how you'll get it. This is where projects most often stall.
3. What you'll have to show at the end (a result, a comparison, a reproduced figure, a review), what would count as success, and what you'll do if it doesn't work.
4. Who you're working with, if anyone.

Peter will read every proposal and reply within 3 days, usually about scope. You can change direction afterward. If you're not sure whether an idea is too big, send it anyway.

## <a name="presentation"></a>Presentation --- Mon, Nov 30 -- Fri, Dec 4

Each project will give an oral presentation with slides in class.

Everyone presents. If you're on a team, each of you speaks for part of the slot.

I am planning on allocating 20 minutes for each project presentation, including questions, though this may shift a bit.  I'll set the exact length and the order once the proposals are in.

Send me your slides as a PDF by 9pm the night before your slot.

## <a name="comments"></a>Comments on classmates' presentations

When you're in the audience, fill out a Google Form for each talk. The form will ask for three things: 
- a quick summary of what the project is about (bullets and phrases are fine)
- something you liked about the talk or project
- a suggestion to make the talk or project better
- if it is ok to share the comments with the project author(s) anonymously 

The comments aren't graded. They're there to help the authors before the report is due.

## <a name="report"></a>Report --- Fri, Dec 18, 2:00pm

The registrar sets the due date for final projects. Ours is Fri, Dec 18 at 2:00pm, during the final exam period. Write it like a workshop paper: 6--8 pages of content plus as much additional space as you'd like for references. You may also have optional supplementary information that is as long as you'd like but I only guarantee that I'll read the main content when grading. Upload the report as a PDF on Gradescope.  Upload one report per team, when working in pairs.
