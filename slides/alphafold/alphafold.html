Idea 2, why (figures/make_triangle_figs.py). Main text, Evoformer section: the two kinds of pair update "are inspired by the necessity of consistency of the pair representation - for a pairwise description of amino acids to be representable as a single 3D structure, many constraints must be satisfied including the triangle inequality on distances". No loss or operation enforces the inequality on the cells (SM 1.6.5-1.6.6, 1.9). Bounds use the rounded distances shown (unrounded: 2.28-12.25 and 2.54-20.03 A). Three points lie in a plane, so the triangle is exact; the dashed circle is every point 7.3 A from H82 (a sphere in 3-D). Ablation: Fig. 4a has no clean "no triangle updates" row. "No triangles, biasing or gating (use axial attention)" removes three things at once (SM 1.13.1): multiplicative updates replaced by plain attention, the triangle bias replaced by relative position, the pair bias in row attention replaced by relative position, and all gating. It costs roughly 4-5 GDT on CASP14 and about 2 lDDT-Ca on recent PDB chains (read off the plot). Main text: networks with only the attention or only the multiplicative update are both highly accurate, and the two together are more accurate (no numbers given).

Idea 2, how. Zoom-in on badge 4 of "One block updates ...": same cell (81, 83), same shades. Alg. 11 ("outgoing" edges), for every cell (i, j): z <- LayerNorm(z); a_ij, b_ij = sigmoid(Linear(z_ij)) (.) Linear(z_ij), 128 numbers each ("learned map"); g_ij = sigmoid(Linear(z_ij)) ("gate"); update_ij = g_ij (.) Linear(LayerNorm(sum_k a_ik (.) b_jk)), added to z_ij (Alg. 6 line 6). (.) is element-wise (SM notation). The sum runs over all k, including k = i and k = j. Alg. 12 ("incoming"): sum_k a_ki (.) b_kj, columns i and j. Alg. 13 (around starting node): (i, j) attends over cells (i, k), score q_ij . k_ik / sqrt(c) plus bias b_jk from the third edge, gated; Alg. 14 (ending node): over (k, j), bias b_ki. Order per block (Alg. 6 lines 6-9): outgoing, incoming, starting node, ending node, dropout 0.25. Main text: the multiplicative update "was developed originally as a more symmetric and cheaper replacement for the attention". Not drawn: the first LayerNorm, dropout.

Answers "aren't distances the same in any frame?": yes, distances between two atoms of one structure are, and a loss built only from those ("dRMSD", SM 1.9.3) is 0 for a mirror image. FAPE instead writes atom j in residue i's coordinates, once in the prediction and once in the truth, and measures the gap between those two points (Alg. 28: x_ij = T_i^-1 o x_j, same for the truth, d_ij = |x_ij - x_ij^true|, FAPE = (1/Z) mean min(d_clamp, d_ij), Z = d_clamp = 10 A). That is superimposing the prediction on the truth at residue i, position and orientation. Toy: 6 atoms 3.8 A apart; prediction = exact mirror image; all 15 atom-atom distances equal. Frames built from neighbouring atoms (AlphaFold uses N, C-alpha, C: Alg. 21); y = x turned 90 deg counterclockwise, the 2-D analogue of the right-hand-rule z, so a mirror flips local y as it flips local z in 3-D (SM 1.9.3 eqs. 12-17). Numbers from figures/make_fape_fig.py: atom 5 is 10.3 A from residue 2 in both, at (9.8, 3.3) vs (9.8, -3.3) in residue 2's frame, 6.7 A apart; unchanged after turning the prediction 125 deg (SM 1.9.2: invariant under rigid motions, not reflections); FAPE over all 6 x 6 pairs 0.45 (clamped).

Final FAPE over all atoms and all backbone + side-chain frames; per-layer auxiliary FAPE on C-alpha atoms and backbone frames only (SM 1.9.2, Alg. 20 lines 17, 28). Invariance under rigid motions, not reflections (SM 1.9.2). Reflection flips only the z-component of local coordinates, FAPE(X, X_reflected) = 2 sum |z| (SM 1.9.3 eqs. 11-17). dRMSD-trained model: bimodal GDT, frequently mirror structures; "FAPE is the main component that ensures the correct chirality" (SM 1.9.3, Suppl. Fig. 9); main text: "the main source of chirality". Other sources: ideal geometry always builds the L form (C-beta "always in the left-handed location"), and the chi-angle loss (SM 1.9.3). Side-chain interactions: main text. Clamp: backbone FAPE clamped at 10 A in 90% of mini-batches, unclamped in 10%; side chains always clamped (SM 1.11.5).

PAE: AF2 calls it e_ij, the C-alpha error of residue j with prediction and truth aligned on residue i's backbone frame (SM 1.9.7). The CASP14 models did not have it: "We also fine-tuned these models after CASP14 to add a pTM prediction objective" (Methods). The name "predicted aligned error (PAE) ... as in AF2" is from Abramson et al. 2024. Fit and r from Fig. 2c legend.
